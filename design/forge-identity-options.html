Forge: palette and mark, three and four ways

Each palette is drawn on a miniature Forge window in light and dark, with the colours the app would actually use. Every text colour passes 4.5:1 on its ground in both modes (measured). The marks are shown where they live: the app icon, the title bar at 32px, the tab at 16px, and beside the name.

Palette

A screen people work in all day: neutral grounds, one accent with a job, states that mean something.
A · Tempered steel My pick
Cool steel greys with one temper blue. When steel is heated it shows a fixed run of colours, straw, then bronze, then blue: Forge's states follow that run, so working is straw, needs-you is bronze, done is blue.

Mark

Shown in temper blue (palette A); each takes whichever accent you pick.
1 · The ingot, true to form My pick
A real ingot is a tapered bar, not a cube. Its top face carries the accent, the front stays ink: one silhouette that holds at 16px and says "made here".
Forge
Forge
Risk: close to a "loading bar" or a gold-bar emoji if the proportions drift; the 16px cut is drawn separately, deeper, to hold.
2 · The struck F
An F built from three forged slabs; the top arm, the one just struck, is the hot one. A monogram that reads as a letter first.
Forge
Forge
Risk: lettermarks are everywhere; with the wordmark beside it, it says "F" twice.
3 · Tempered bar
A slanted bar in the temper run, straw to bronze to blue: the mark is palette A's state colours, so the app's states and its logo are one idea.
Forge
Forge
Risk: four colours; it only makes sense with palette A, and stripes can read as a progress bar or a flag.
Today · The cube
For comparison: the isometric cube, top face hot. It is the "box" icon every package manager uses, and at 16px it becomes a hexagon.
